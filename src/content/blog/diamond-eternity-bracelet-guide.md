*An eternity bracelet is a circle of diamonds with no beginning and no end — the design concept is simple, the execution is not.*

---

### The Concept
## Why Continuous Diamond Coverage Is Both the Strength and the Challenge

An eternity bracelet sets diamonds through the full circumference of the piece — the underside of the wrist carries the same stones as the top. This contrasts with a bracelet designed with a stone-free underside, which reduces cost but also breaks the visual continuity. The eternity configuration means that at any point of rotation during wear, diamonds are visible. This is the design's core proposition.

The fabrication challenge is identical to the eternity ring but scaled to wrist circumference: the stones must match in size, color, and setting height across the full run, including the sections that sit against the inner wrist and are rarely seen. A jeweler who cuts corners on the underside stone quality of an eternity piece is selling you a partial eternity masquerading as a full one. Ask specifically whether the piece is full-eternity before purchasing.

The mathematics of a full eternity bracelet are unforgiving. A 7-inch wrist circumference bracelet set with 3.5mm round brilliant diamonds will require exactly the number of stones that fit that circumference without gaps or crowding. Unlike a ring, which can be designed in standard finger sizes, each wrist bracelet circumference requires its own stone count and may require stones cut to a specific calibrated diameter if the design is to work cleanly. This is why custom eternity bracelets take longer than eternity rings — the tolerance for error in the stone count is essentially zero.

### The Settings
## How Channel, Bezel, and Prong Configurations Change the Piece

Channel settings are the most technically appropriate choice for an eternity bracelet. In a channel setting, the diamonds sit in a groove between two parallel rails of metal. The rails hold each stone from above and below, and the stones share the channel walls with their neighbors. The result is a clean, continuous surface of diamonds with minimal visible metal — from the top, the bracelet reads as an uninterrupted ribbon of stone.

The engineering precision required for channel setting in a bracelet is substantial. Each diamond must be calibrated to the same size within 0.1mm so that the channel walls make clean, even contact with every stone simultaneously. One stone that is slightly taller or shorter than its neighbors creates a step in the channel wall that catches light differently and reads as a visible inconsistency. The channel must be cut to the correct depth — too shallow and the stones sit proud of the metal and risk impact damage; too deep and the stones are recessed and lose light entry from the sides.

Prong settings in eternity bracelets produce higher brilliance than channel settings, because each stone's sides are open to ambient light. The tradeoff is the presence of prong tips around every stone — hundreds of tips across the full bracelet circumference — each of which must be inspected annually and retipped as needed. For a bracelet worn daily, this maintenance commitment is real and should factor into the decision. Bezel-set eternity bracelets are rare because the individual bezels required for each stone through the full circumference add metal bulk that can overwhelm the stone surface area at this scale.

### The Fit
## Why Sizing an Eternity Bracelet Is Not Like Sizing a Regular Bracelet

A regular bracelet adjusts at the clasp — links are added or removed, and the fit is precise regardless of the original size. An eternity bracelet with diamonds through its full circumference cannot simply have a link added without sourcing a matching stone, setting it, and integrating it seamlessly into the channel or prong run. This means sizing an eternity bracelet after fabrication is possible but significantly more expensive and complex than a standard bracelet adjustment.

The right approach is to have the piece sized correctly at fabrication. We measure the wrist at the point where the bracelet will be worn — typically just above the wrist bone — and add a quarter inch for comfortable wear. For a full-eternity channel bracelet, we design the stone run to accommodate that specific circumference, which may require slight adjustment in the channel width or the stone diameter to ensure a clean fit.

The clasp on a full-eternity bracelet is the one non-diamond element in the design. On quality pieces, the clasp is built as a separate section that matches the bracelet's profile and width, contains no stones (which would be inaccessible to the clasp mechanism), and opens with a secure box-clasp mechanism. The clasp section should be as inconspicuous as the design allows. Some clients prefer a clasp set with diamonds on the outer face only, which maintains the visual continuity from the top view while keeping the inner face clear for the clasp mechanism to function.

### What People
## Ask Us

**Is a full eternity bracelet really set with diamonds on the underside?**
A genuine full eternity bracelet carries diamonds through the complete circumference — top, sides, and underside. Half-eternity or three-quarter-eternity bracelets set diamonds on the top and sides only. The distinction affects both price and the design's visual integrity. Ask the seller to confirm in writing whether the piece is full or partial eternity, and examine the underside before purchasing.

**What stone shapes work in eternity bracelets?**
Round brilliants are the most practical because their circular profile fits neatly in calibrated channel settings. Oval, cushion, and princess cuts also work in channel configurations when calibrated to consistent dimensions. Fancy shapes with irregular outlines — marquise, pear, hexagon — are significantly more difficult to fit in a continuous eternity configuration and require more generous metal framing between stones.

**How do I prevent stones from becoming loose in a channel bracelet?**
Annual inspection and cleaning are the primary preventive measures. Stones become loose in channel settings when the channel rail above the stone wears thin from daily contact, allowing the stone to move. A jeweler can tighten the channel by pressing the rail back toward the stone — this is a routine maintenance operation on any channel-set piece. Catching a loose stone early prevents loss.

**Can an eternity bracelet be worn with a watch?**
Yes. The most common configuration is the eternity bracelet on the same wrist as the watch, positioned above the watch on the forearm side. This keeps the bracelet visible and prevents the bracelet from sliding over the watch crystal. The bracelet should be close-fitted enough that it does not slide to the watch position during normal wrist movement.

**What total carat weight does an eternity bracelet typically carry?**
A 7-inch circumference eternity bracelet with 3.5mm round brilliants carries approximately sixty stones at roughly 0.10 carats each — six total carats. Smaller stones at 2.5mm diameter carry less weight per stone and allow more stones per inch, producing a more delicate, lace-like surface. Larger stones at 4.5mm produce fewer stones per inch and a more substantial, bold look. Total carat weight on eternity bracelets ranges from four to over twelve carats depending on stone size and diameter.
