*Wedding rings, anniversary rings, and eternity rings each mark a different chapter. Understanding the distinctions helps you choose something that actually means what you intend it to.*

---

Three rings, three conversations. They overlap in style and sometimes in function, but they carry different intentions. A wedding band is a promise made once, worn closest to the heart. An anniversary ring marks time and survival. An eternity ring is a statement about permanence. Knowing which you are choosing, and why, produces a better result than browsing without that clarity.

### The Original
## Wedding Band

The wedding band is the foundational piece. Worn on the ring finger of the left hand, it sits beneath the engagement ring when both are worn together, placing it closest to the heart as the older tradition holds.

Plain bands run in gold, platinum, and palladium. A simple 2mm band in yellow gold reads differently than a 5mm flat band in platinum, and both read differently than a knife-edge profile that catches light along its ridge. Width, profile, and metal choice together determine how the band presents on the hand.

Diamond wedding bands add another variable. A pavé band, where small diamonds run along the exterior of the shank, pairs naturally with most engagement ring styles. A channel-set band, where diamonds sit in a groove between two rails of metal, has a cleaner, more architectural line. The choice between them often comes down to whether a person prefers sparkle or structure.

Bez Ambar's bands are made to stack. The Knife-Edge band, with its pointed profile, creates a specific interaction with the engagement ring above it: a deliberate gap that lets each piece breathe rather than pressing flat. The Bookend band frames the engagement ring on either side. Both approaches require some planning, because the wedding band and engagement ring need to work together as a composed set.

### For Milestones
## The Anniversary Ring

An anniversary ring is a gift from one partner to the other, usually marking a significant year: fifth, tenth, twenty-fifth. There is no rule about what it must look like, but the intention is usually to add to what already exists rather than replace it.

Three-stone rings carry symbolic weight in this context. The three stones represent past, present, and future, which maps naturally onto a long marriage. A large center stone flanked by two slightly smaller ones makes this composition read clearly.

A matching band, identical or complementary to the original wedding band, is another common approach. After twenty years the original band may show wear, and adding a second band alongside it creates a layered effect that reads as accumulated time rather than a simple upgrade.

The anniversary ring does not need to be announced. It can be a private gift, worn on the right hand or stacked with the existing set. What matters is that it acknowledges something specific.

### All the Way Around
## The Eternity Ring

An eternity ring has diamonds or gemstones set all the way around the band, with no plain metal visible from any angle. This is a different construction than a half-eternity band, where stones cover only the visible top half of the shank. The distinction matters both visually and practically.

A true eternity band cannot be resized, because cutting the shank to add or remove metal would destroy the continuous stone setting. Sizing has to be right from the start. For many people this is a minor consideration; for those whose finger size fluctuates significantly, it is worth thinking through.

Eternity bands in pavé, channel, and bead settings each have a different texture. Pavé has the most light return because each stone sits high and exposed. Channel-set eternity bands have a lower, smoother profile. Bead-set bands, where each stone is held by small raised beads of metal rather than prongs or rails, have a distinctive handmade quality.

### What People
## Ask Us

**What is the difference between a wedding ring and an eternity ring?**
A wedding ring is the band exchanged at the ceremony. It may be plain or have diamonds, but it typically has some plain metal in the shank. An eternity ring has stones set continuously around the entire band. They are structurally different, and an eternity ring cannot be resized after setting, which a standard wedding band usually can.

**When do people typically give an anniversary ring?**
The most common milestones are the fifth, tenth, and twenty-fifth anniversaries, though there is no rule. Some couples give anniversary rings at any year that feels significant to them. It is a gift between partners rather than a ceremony piece, so the timing is personal.

**Can an eternity band be resized?**
True eternity bands, where stones run all the way around, cannot be resized without destroying the setting. Half-eternity bands, where stones cover only the top half of the shank, can sometimes be resized depending on the metal and how the stones are set. When ordering an eternity band, get the sizing right from the start.

**What metal works best for a wedding band meant to last decades?**
Platinum is the most durable choice for a band worn every day. It does not wear away like gold does; instead it develops a patina called a "patina" where metal moves rather than disappears. Yellow and rose gold in 18k are also durable and can be polished back to their original finish. White gold requires rhodium replating over time to maintain its bright white color.

**Should my wedding band match my engagement ring exactly?**
Not necessarily. Many people prefer a complementary relationship rather than an exact match. The Knife-Edge band, for example, creates an intentional gap with many engagement ring profiles rather than sitting flush. The question is whether you want the two pieces to read as one composed set or as distinct pieces worn together.
