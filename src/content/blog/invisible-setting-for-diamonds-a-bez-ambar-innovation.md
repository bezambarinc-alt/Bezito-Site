*In a standard diamond setting, you can see the metal holding the stone — prongs gripping it, a bezel surrounding it, channels containing it. In an invisible setting, you cannot. The diamonds appear to rest against each other with nothing holding them in place. This effect takes extraordinary precision to produce.*

### What Invisible Setting Actually Is
## The Technique Explained

Invisible setting — sometimes called mystery setting, a term originally associated with Van Cleef & Arpels — is a method of securing diamonds in a metal framework without any visible metal crossing the face of the stones.

The standard approach uses small grooves or channels cut into the underside of each diamond. The metal framework has corresponding rails that slide into these grooves, locking the stones in place from below and at the sides. Viewed from above, you see only diamonds: a continuous surface of facets with no prong tips, no bezel walls, nothing interrupting the field of light.

The engineering challenge is significant. Each diamond must be cut to precise tolerances — typically within a fraction of a millimeter — to fit into the framework correctly. The grooves in each stone must be cut without weakening the stone's structure. The framework must be designed to hold dozens of identically cut stones in perfect alignment while remaining completely invisible from the viewing angle.

### Why It Is Difficult
## The Precision Required

In a prong setting, the jeweler has flexibility. The prong can be bent slightly to accommodate a stone that is marginally larger or smaller than specified. A bezel can be pushed down around a stone that sits slightly high. These micro-adjustments are routine in standard setting work.

Invisible setting allows no such adjustments. Every stone must be correct before it goes into the piece. The framework geometry is fixed. If a stone is 0.05mm too wide, it will not seat properly against its neighbors. If the grooves are cut at the wrong angle, the stone will not engage the rail.

The stones in an invisible setting are typically princess cut (square brilliant) diamonds, because the square girdle makes the groove-cutting feasible. Round brilliants have a curved girdle that makes the rail engagement geometrically complex — possible, but far rarer and more expensive.

Each stone in a Bez Ambar invisible setting is cut in-house to the specifications of that specific piece. This is not standard procedure in the industry; most jewelry manufacturers source pre-cut stones and adjust the setting design accordingly. Cutting to the setting reverses that process — the design dictates the stone specifications, and the stones are cut to match.

### Invisible Setting at Bez Ambar
## Where You Will See It

Invisible setting appears throughout the Bez Ambar collection in several applications:

**Bracelets and bangles:** A continuous row or field of invisibly set princess cut diamonds creating an unbroken surface of light around the wrist. The bracelet appears to be made of nothing but diamonds.

**Ring shanks:** Invisible setting along the shank of an engagement ring or wedding band, where the side profile shows a seamless field of diamonds with no visible metal between them.

**Earrings:** Square or rectangular earring faces set with invisible-set diamonds produce a mirror-like surface of pure brilliance without the visual interruption of metal.

**Pendants:** Geometric shapes — squares, triangles, rectangles — where invisible setting allows the shape to be executed in pure diamond surface.

The pieces require longer production time than prong-set equivalents and carry a premium that reflects both the material cost (stones must be cut to specification) and the labor (setting time for invisible work is three to five times longer than standard prong or channel setting).

### Caring for Invisibly Set Jewelry
## What to Know

Invisible setting is structurally secure under normal wearing conditions, but it has specific vulnerabilities that prong-set pieces do not.

**Avoid impact.** The rails holding the stones engage from below. A direct sharp impact against the face of an invisibly set piece can dislodge stones that would stay in place under the same impact in a prong setting. This is a structural feature of the technique, not a flaw in any specific piece.

**Professional cleaning only.** Ultrasonic cleaners are generally not recommended for invisibly set pieces. The vibration can loosen the rail engagement over time. Professional hand cleaning under low pressure is the correct approach.

**Annual inspection.** Like any fine jewelry, invisibly set pieces should be inspected annually by a jeweler who is familiar with the construction. Loose stones in an invisible setting cannot be detected by sight — you will not see a stone sitting slightly high the way you would with a loose prong-set diamond. An experienced jeweler checks by touch and under magnification.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is invisible setting in jewelry?**
Invisible setting is a technique in which diamonds are secured by a hidden metal framework — rails that engage grooves cut into the underside of each stone — so that no metal is visible from above. The result is a continuous surface of diamonds with no prongs, bezels, or channels interrupting the field of light.

**Is invisible setting durable?**
Yes, under normal wearing conditions. Invisibly set diamonds are held securely by the rail-and-groove system. The construction is vulnerable to sharp direct impact on the face of the piece — more so than prong-set stones — so pieces should be removed for high-impact activities. Annual professional inspection is recommended.

**Why is invisible setting expensive?**
Two reasons: material and labor. Every stone must be cut to precise specifications for that piece, which means dedicated cutting time rather than sourcing from commodity inventory. The setting time is three to five times longer than standard prong or channel work. Both of these costs pass through to the finished piece.

**Can any diamond shape be invisibly set?**
Princess cut (square brilliant) diamonds are most commonly used because the square girdle allows clean groove cutting. Round brilliants can be invisibly set but the curved girdle makes the technique significantly more complex. Baguettes and other rectangular cuts are also used, particularly in row-set applications.
