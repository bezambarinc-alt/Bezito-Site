*Pave diamonds multiply a ring's light from every angle — but the setting technique and your maintenance habits determine whether that brilliance lasts a lifetime or fades within years.*

---

### What Pave Is
## Understanding the Setting Before You Buy

Pave — from the French word for "paved" — describes a method of setting small diamonds so closely together that the metal beneath them nearly disappears. The result is a continuous surface of stone, where light bounces between diamonds and the effect is exponentially brighter than any single stone can achieve alone.

There are three primary pave constructions in serious jewelry. Standard pave uses small beads of metal, raised from the shank itself, to secure each stone. Micro-pave tightens the spacing even further, using stones typically under 1.5mm in diameter and prongs so fine they require magnification to inspect. French pave, also called fish-tail pave, cuts a V-shaped channel between each stone to allow light to enter from the side, producing a sharper, more faceted look across the band. Each method has a different light character and a different durability profile — something most buyers never hear before the purchase.

A well-executed pave setting has each stone sitting at exactly the same depth, with no raised edges or tilted tables. Run your fingertip across a properly set pave band and it should feel nearly smooth. Roughness or catching means the setting is uneven — a problem that compounds with wear.

### Why Light Matters
## How Diamond Placement Controls Brilliance

The mathematics of pave are precise. Each small diamond is a complete optical engine — it reflects, refracts, and returns light to the eye. When stones are placed too far apart, the metal between them absorbs light rather than redirecting it. When stones are set too close without adequate prong support, they shift under pressure and their light axes diverge, dulling the surface.

Forty years of setting pave has taught me that the critical variable is depth consistency. A stone sitting 0.1mm lower than its neighbors will appear dark against the field because its crown facets are no longer catching the same ambient light. No amount of cleaning recovers that loss. The fix is resetting — a service most jewelers will not volunteer unless you know to ask.

For engagement rings specifically, the shank curvature matters as much as the flat surface. Diamonds set on a curved surface must be cut to a slightly smaller diameter than those on the same ring's flat sections, or they will seat at subtly different angles. Experienced pave setters account for this automatically. Inexperienced ones do not.

### Caring for Pave
## Maintenance That Preserves the Setting Long-Term

Pave is not delicate, but it rewards attention. The prongs that hold micro-pave stones are measured in fractions of a millimeter. They wear. Chlorine accelerates that wear dramatically, which is why pave rings should be removed before swimming pools, hot tubs, and cleaning with bleach-based products. Ultrasonic cleaners, used without supervision, can vibrate stones loose — particularly in settings where a prong has already started to thin.

A professional inspection once a year is the minimum. The jeweler checks prong height under magnification and re-tips any that have worn below safe holding depth. Catching a failing prong before the stone is lost is a ten-minute repair. Replacing a lost micro-pave stone requires sourcing a calibrated match, resetting, and sometimes refinishing the surrounding metal. It is not catastrophic, but it is avoidable.

Home cleaning for pave is straightforward. Warm water, a drop of mild dish soap, and a soft child's toothbrush applied gently to the underside of the setting where lotion and soap accumulate. Rinse thoroughly and dry with a lint-free cloth. Do this monthly and the stones will maintain their fire between professional cleanings.

### Choosing the Metal
## Metal Hardness Changes Every Pave Outcome

Platinum and white gold are the standard metal choices for pave settings, and they behave differently in ways that affect the setting's longevity. Platinum is denser and more malleable — it moves under impact rather than cracking, which protects prongs. Over time, platinum develops a patina that can slightly obscure the brightness of micro-pave stones until the ring is polished. White gold is harder in its alloyed state, holds a high polish longer, but requires periodic rhodium plating to maintain its white color, and the plating process should never contact prong tips carelessly.

Yellow gold pave exists and can be stunning in the right design context, but it demands a higher prong-to-stone ratio because gold's softness means prongs compress faster. Any jeweler recommending 18K yellow gold micro-pave for daily wear without discussing this trade-off is leaving out essential information.

The metal choice should be made alongside the setting style, not after. They are one decision.

### What People
## Ask Us

**How many diamonds are typically in a pave engagement ring shank?**
The count depends on shank width and stone diameter, but a standard 2mm shank with micro-pave running halfway around the band typically holds between 28 and 40 stones. A full eternity pave band doubles that. The number matters less than the total carat weight and the consistency of the setting — a tight, well-set 30-stone shank outperforms a loosely set 50-stone one in every optical measure.

**Can pave stones fall out during normal daily wear?**
A properly executed pave setting should not lose stones under normal daily wear. Stone loss almost always traces back to one of three causes: a prong that was set too thin at manufacture, a prong that wore down without inspection, or an impact sharp enough to compress the metal laterally. Annual inspections catch the first two causes before they become losses. Avoiding sharp lateral impacts — the edge of a gym weight, a car door frame — addresses the third.

**Is micro-pave harder to maintain than standard pave?**
Yes, in proportion to the prong size. Micro-pave prongs are finer and wear faster, which means the inspection interval matters more, not less. That said, micro-pave is not fragile on a well-made ring — it simply requires an owner who understands the maintenance commitment. The trade-off is a surface brightness that standard pave cannot match.

**What is the difference between pave and channel-set diamonds?**
Channel setting places diamonds inside a continuous metal groove, with the channel walls holding the stones rather than individual prongs. Channel setting is more protective of the stones and slightly more resistant to catching on fabric, but it allows no light to enter from the sides. Pave, with its individual prongs and open metal between stones, produces more fire and scintillation because light reaches the diamonds from multiple angles. Neither is superior in absolute terms — the choice depends on the wearer's aesthetic preference and how much they want to trade brilliance for durability.

**Does pave add significant carat weight to a ring?**
In practical terms, yes, though the individual stones are small. A full micro-pave eternity band in a 2mm width typically adds 0.50 to 0.80 carats total. A half-pave shank adds 0.25 to 0.40 carats. These numbers compound meaningfully when comparing total ring cost, and they are worth discussing explicitly with your jeweler before a purchase is finalized so there are no surprises on the invoice.
