**The idea behind a halo setting is simple:** surround your center stone with smaller diamonds, and the eye reads the whole composition as one larger stone.

The execution is where most jewelers stop short.

---

## The Problem with the Standard Halo

The conventional approach — a row of small round diamonds set around the center stone in tiny prong mounts — works. The light increases. The ring reads larger.

But look closely and you see the gaps. Between every stone, there are small slivers of metal. The halo has seams.

At distance it reads fine. At arm's length, in good light, you notice what's missing.

---

## What Bez Ambar Did Differently

Bez Ambar spent years solving the seam problem.

The answer was to abandon round accent stones entirely. Instead, each stone in the Blaze® halo is custom-cut square — and individually shaped to sit flush against its neighbor with no gaps between them.

The result is an unbroken ring of light around the center stone. Not almost unbroken. Unbroken.

From a normal viewing distance, the halo and the center diamond fuse. You cannot tell where one ends and the other begins. The stone appears — genuinely appears — to be larger than it is.

## What a Halo Actually Does to the Size

A well-executed halo adds roughly 0.25 to 0.50 carats of visual weight to the center stone. Not actual weight — visual weight. The difference is important, and meaningful.

A 1.00-carat diamond in a Blaze® halo reads closer to 1.40 carats. The price difference between those two stones, in equivalent quality, can be substantial. The halo closes that gap for a fraction of the cost.

## Styles Worth Knowing

**Single halo** — One row of accent stones. Clean. Works with every center shape. The version most people picture.

**Double halo** — Two concentric rows. More elaborate, more visual size. Best on round and cushion centers.

**Hidden halo** — Accent diamonds set beneath the center stone's basket, invisible from above. The stone sparkles in ways you can't explain at first glance.

**Colored halo** — Sapphires or rubies replacing white diamonds as the accent stones. A colored halo adds personality and contrast without competing with the center stone.

---

## Which Center Stones Work Best

Every shape benefits from a halo. The effect varies.

Round brilliants and cushion cuts integrate most naturally — the soft geometry of the halo mirrors the stone's own silhouette. Ovals and pears gain visual length. Princess cuts pair cleanly with a square halo frame.

The stone doesn't need to be exceptional to carry a halo well. That, in a sense, is the point.

---

## Is It Right for You?

A halo rewards anyone who wants presence on the finger without the cost of a significantly larger stone. It suits detailed, considered jewelry — not minimalism.

If you want the center stone to stand alone, unframed and singular, a solitaire will always be the correct answer. The halo is for those who want something more.

## Questions We're Often Asked

**Does a halo setting actually make a diamond look bigger?**
Yes — and meaningfully so. A single halo adds roughly 0.25–0.50 carats of visual weight. The Blaze® halo maximizes this by eliminating the gaps between accent stones, so the halo fuses with the center stone rather than framing it from a distance.

**What makes the Blaze® halo different from a standard pavé halo?**
A pavé halo uses round diamonds with visible metal gaps between each stone. The Blaze® uses custom-cut square diamonds, each shaped to fit flush against the next, with no gaps. The difference is visible the moment you see them side by side.
