**TL;DR:** A halo ring setting frames your center diamond with a border of smaller accent stones. It visually enlarges the center stone by 0.25–0.50 carats in appearance — often for a fraction of the cost of buying a larger stone. The two main styles are pavé halo (round melee diamonds, gaps between stones) and Blaze® halo (custom-cut square diamonds, no gaps, seamless brilliance). Bez Ambar invented the Blaze® setting and holds the design patent.

---

## What Is a Halo Ring Setting?

A halo ring setting places a frame of smaller diamonds around the center stone of an engagement ring. The accent stones amplify light return around the center diamond, creating the visual impression of a much larger stone.

The effect isn't illusion in the cheap sense — it's optics. Light bouncing off dozens of small facets fuses with light bouncing off the center stone. At conversational distance, where rings are actually seen, the eye reads the whole cluster as one large glittering mass.

This matters because diamond pricing is not linear. A 1.50-carat round brilliant costs roughly four times more than a 1.00-carat stone of equivalent quality. A halo setting can make that 1.00-carat stone look like 1.25–1.40 carats. The math almost always favors the halo.

## The Two Types of Halo You Need to Know

### Pavé Halo

The traditional approach. Small round diamonds (called melée stones) are set around the center stone, each held by tiny shared prongs. The prong metal creates small visible gaps between the accent stones, breaking the band of light around the center diamond.

Pavé halos are widely available, well-understood, and work beautifully in classic solitaire designs. The gaps are a technical limitation, not a design failure.

### Blaze® Halo — The Bez Ambar Innovation

Bez Ambar engineered a fundamentally different approach. Instead of round melée diamonds, the Blaze® halo uses custom-cut square diamonds — each one individually cut to fit flush against its neighbor and shaped to match the geometry of the center stone.

Because square stones have flat edges, they seat edge-to-edge with no gaps. The result is an unbroken perimeter of brilliance that visually merges with the center stone. From any reasonable viewing angle, it becomes difficult to identify where the halo ends and the center stone begins.

Bez Ambar received a design patent for the Blaze® setting. It is only available through Bez Ambar directly or through an authorized Bez Ambar retailer — it cannot be replicated by other jewelers.

## How Much Bigger Does a Halo Setting Make a Diamond Look?

On average, a single halo adds approximately 0.25–0.50 carats of visual weight to a center stone. The exact gain depends on:

- **Stone shape** — Round and cushion centers benefit most; elongated shapes (oval, pear, marquise) gain less proportionally but still benefit
- **Halo width** — A wider halo adds more visual size; a slim halo adds elegance without dramatically changing the silhouette
- **Gap vs. seamless** — A seamless halo like Blaze® maximizes the size illusion; a pavé halo with gaps is more modest in its effect
- **Metal color** — White gold or platinum halos blend into the center stone; yellow or rose gold halos create more contrast, emphasizing the center stone separately

## Halo Setting Styles Worth Considering

**Classic single halo** — One row of accent diamonds around the center stone. Works with virtually every center stone shape. Clean, timeless.

**Double halo** — Two concentric rows of accent stones. Adds significant visual size and a more elaborate aesthetic. Best suited to round and cushion centers.

**Hidden halo** — Accent diamonds set on the underside of the center stone's basket, visible only when the ring is viewed from the side or bottom. Adds sparkle without changing the top-down silhouette.

**Floral or shaped halo** — The halo follows a decorative outline (petals, geometric shapes) rather than mirroring the center stone. A specialty design that requires skilled execution.

**Colored stone halo** — Sapphires, rubies, or fancy colored diamonds as the halo accent stones. Adds color contrast and personality. Popularized partly by Princess Diana's sapphire engagement ring.

## The Right Center Stone for a Halo Setting

Halo settings work with every center stone shape. The visual effect varies:

- **Round brilliant** — Most popular with halo; the circular halo matches the stone's symmetry perfectly
- **Cushion cut** — Works beautifully; the soft corners blend naturally into the halo border
- **Oval** — Elongated halos add visual length to the finger
- **Princess cut** — Square halo or rotated square halo; aligns with the corner geometry
- **Pear, marquise, emerald** — Matched halos that follow the stone outline; requires precise craftsmanship

## Is a Halo Setting Right for You?

A halo is the right choice if:
- You want maximum visual size within a set budget
- You appreciate intricate, detailed jewelry design
- Your center stone is high-quality but modest in carat weight

A halo may not be right if:
- You prefer a clean, minimal look where the center stone stands alone
- You have an exceptionally large center stone (where the halo diminishes it)
- You want a design that ages easily without requiring precise cleaning of accent stones

Halo settings require slightly more maintenance than solitaires — the accent stones can accumulate residue in the gaps between prongs. Regular cleaning and an annual check by a jeweler to confirm all prongs are secure is good practice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a halo setting make a diamond look bigger?**
Yes. A well-executed halo adds 0.25–0.50 carats of visual weight to the center stone. The seamless Blaze® halo by Bez Ambar maximizes this effect by eliminating gaps between accent diamonds.

**What is the difference between a pavé halo and a Blaze® halo?**
A pavé halo uses round diamonds separated by small prong gaps. The Blaze® halo (patented by Bez Ambar) uses custom-cut square diamonds set edge-to-edge with no gaps, creating an unbroken band of brilliance around the center stone.

**Does a halo engagement ring look dated?**
Halo settings have been in continuous production since the Georgian era — they are not a trend, they are a category. The specific style of halo (slim vs. wide, pavé vs. seamless, single vs. double) does evolve, but the form itself is enduring.

**How much does a halo setting add to the price of a ring?**
A standard pavé halo adds roughly $500–$2,000 to the cost of a setting depending on the total carat weight of accent diamonds and the quality of the metalwork. A Blaze® halo costs more because each accent stone is custom-cut — but the result is visually worth it.

**Can you add a halo to an existing ring?**
In most cases, yes. A skilled jeweler can design a halo that sits around your existing center stone and attaches to a new or modified setting. The feasibility depends on the current setting's architecture. Contact us to discuss retrofitting your stone.

**Where can I buy a Blaze® halo ring?**
