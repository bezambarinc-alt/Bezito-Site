The price of a diamond does not increase in a straight line. It jumps. A 1.00-carat round brilliant in G/VS2 might be priced at $6,000. The same quality at 2.00 carats might be $28,000 or more. The visual difference between those two stones on the hand is real but far smaller than the price difference would suggest.

This is the problem the Blaze® halo solves. Not by faking size, but by completing it.

### The Gap
## Between Price and Size

When you buy a larger center stone, you are not just paying for the extra diamond weight. You are paying for the rarity of finding a stone of that weight that still meets quality standards. Diamond prices per carat increase exponentially as size increases, because larger rough diamonds are exponentially rarer than smaller ones.

A 0.90-carat diamond and a 1.10-carat diamond look nearly identical on the hand. But the price gap between them can be 20 to 30 percent. This is the carat-weight premium at work: the market assigns significant value to crossing the 1.00-carat threshold, even though the visual return is minimal.

The question then becomes: where should the money go?

### An Optical
## Fact

A halo works by surrounding the center stone with smaller diamonds. The eye, unable to locate exactly where the center stone ends and the halo begins, reads the entire composition as one continuous, larger stone. This is not a trick. The light is real. The brilliance is real. The perception is accurate within its own logic.

What determines how effective this illusion is comes down to one thing: the gaps.

In a conventional pavé halo, small round diamonds sit in prong settings. Between each stone, a sliver of metal is visible. Under light, these slivers interrupt the halo's continuity. The eye catches the seams, and the illusion weakens at the precise moment it should be strongest.

A standard pavé halo adds roughly 0.20 to 0.25 carats of perceived size to the center stone. Better than nothing, but not the full potential.

### No Gaps
## The Blaze® Difference

The Blaze® accent stone is square, not round. Each stone is individually cut to fit its position in the halo, shaped to match the curvature of the center stone it surrounds. When set, these stones sit edge to edge. No prong gaps. No metal interruptions. No visible seams.

The halo closes, completely.

What results is a continuous perimeter of light that fuses with the center stone rather than framing it from a distance. A 1.00-carat diamond in a Blaze® halo reads closer to 1.40 carats on the hand. The difference between a 1.00-carat and a 1.40-carat stone at equivalent quality represents a cost of many thousands of dollars. The Blaze® produces that perception for a fraction of that cost.

This is not a compromise. The center stone is exactly what it is: a well-chosen diamond with a verified certificate. The Blaze® simply completes what it is capable of looking like.

### Where to Put
## the Budget

The practical implication of this is straightforward. Given a fixed budget, you have a choice: spend it on a larger center stone, or spend it on a smaller center stone in a Blaze® halo that reads as large or larger.

The larger center stone without a halo has one advantage: simplicity. A solitaire is a clean statement, and its apparent size grows or shrinks based entirely on the stone.

The Blaze® halo has a different advantage: a 1.00-carat stone in a Blaze® setting carries more visual presence than a 1.40-carat stone in no setting at all. The design does work that the stone alone cannot do.

For most buyers, the better allocation of a fixed budget is a higher-quality 1.00-carat stone in a Blaze® halo rather than a lower-quality larger stone in a plain solitaire. You get more brilliance, more perceived size, and a patented design that no other jeweler can offer.

### What People
## Ask Us

**How much bigger does a Blaze® halo make the center stone look?**
A 1.00-carat diamond in a Blaze® halo reads approximately 1.35 to 1.45 carats on the hand, depending on the center stone's shape and the specific halo configuration. The standard pavé halo typically adds 0.20 to 0.25 carats of perceived size. The Blaze® adds more because there are no gaps between accent stones breaking the visual continuity.

**Is it better to buy a bigger stone or use a smaller stone in a Blaze® halo?**
For most people, a well-cut 1.00-carat stone in a Blaze® halo outperforms a 1.40-carat stone in a plain solitaire in terms of visual presence and total light return. Whether that is the right trade depends on whether you want the design to contribute to the ring's presence or want the stone to speak entirely on its own. Both are valid. The Blaze® simply offers a different path to the same visual destination.

**Does the Blaze® halo add significantly to the cost of the ring?**
The Blaze® costs more than a standard pavé halo because each accent stone is custom-cut for its position rather than selected from standard inventory. But the cost increment is substantially less than the cost of upgrading the center stone to achieve equivalent perceived size.

**Can the Blaze® halo be used with any center stone shape?**
Yes. Bez Ambar designs Blaze® halos around round brilliants, ovals, cushion cuts, princess cuts, and other shapes. Each halo configuration is engineered specifically for the geometry of the center stone it surrounds. The result is always a close fit, because the stones are cut to order.
