*The men's wedding band is worn on the same finger every day for decades — it is the most worn piece of jewelry most men will ever own, and the decisions around width, metal, finish, and thickness determine whether that experience is comfortable or not.*

---

### The Width
## Why Width Is the First Decision and Not the Last

Band width is the dimension most men choose last and most regret first. Width is measured in millimeters across the band's face — the distance from one edge to the other. Most men's bands run from 4mm to 10mm, with 6mm and 8mm being the two most common selections. The width a band feels like on the hand is not always the width a band looks like in a display case, and this discrepancy is the most common source of post-purchase regret.

Wider bands — above 8mm — impose a structural constraint on finger movement. The wedding band sits at the base of the ring finger; a wide band extends up the finger away from the knuckle and can restrict the finger's natural range of motion when the hand is closed into a fist. This is not a problem for every wearer, but it is a problem for men who work with their hands, play instruments, or are simply sensitive to the sensation of restriction. Try a wide band before committing.

The proportionality rule for band width is that the band's visual presence on the hand should be calibrated to the wearer's hand and finger size. A 4mm band on a wide hand with substantial fingers reads as a fine wire and provides almost no visual presence. A 10mm band on a narrow hand reads as disproportionately large. The target is a band that reads as intentional and resolved relative to the hand it is on — not so fine that it disappears, not so wide that it dominates. For most average adult male hands, 6mm to 8mm hits this proportional target.

### The Metal
## Platinum, Gold, and Alternative Metals — What Each Provides

Men's bands are made in a wider range of metals than women's fine jewelry, and the choice has genuine consequences for durability, comfort, and long-term appearance. The three categories to understand are precious metals (platinum, gold in various karats), contemporary metals (titanium, cobalt chrome, tungsten carbide), and mixed constructions that combine elements of both.

Platinum is the most appropriate metal for a men's wedding band intended for lifetime daily wear. It does not change color, does not require plating, and is the most durable of the precious metals at equivalent gauge. Platinum develops a wear patina — a subtle surface texture that replaces the initial high polish — which many men prefer to the alternative of periodically polishing the band back to mirror finish. The patina is reversible; the band can be polished professionally at any time.

Gold in 14k or 18k is the traditional precious metal choice. Yellow gold reads richly and does not require surface maintenance other than occasional polishing. White gold requires rhodium replating to maintain its white color, which is a maintenance consideration for a band worn daily in demanding environments — workshop, kitchen, athletic training. Rose gold reads warmly and personally, and is the most distinctive of the three gold color options.

Titanium, tungsten, and cobalt chrome are marketed as durable alternatives that are harder than precious metals and available at significantly lower price points. They are genuinely harder than gold. They are also not resizable — resizing precious metal bands is a routine bench operation, but titanium and tungsten bands cannot be resized and must be replaced if the finger size changes. For a ring worn every day for decades, this is a significant practical constraint.

### The Finish
## Surface Treatment and What It Says About the Piece

A wedding band's surface finish determines both its appearance and its maintenance requirements. High-polish finish produces a mirror surface that reflects light sharply and reads as formal and classic. Brushed or matte finish produces a directional satin texture that absorbs and diffuses light and reads as more understated and contemporary. Hammered finish creates an irregular, textured surface that catches light unpredictably and reads as handcrafted.

Combination finishes — polished edges with a brushed face, or hammered exterior with polished inner channel — add visual interest without the full visual commitment of an all-over texture. A polished center stripe on a brushed band is the most common combination finish and works well on bands from 6mm upward where there is sufficient width to separate the two surface regions clearly.

The maintenance implication of high-polish finish is that every contact scratch is visible against the mirror surface. Men who wear their bands through work, sports, or hands-on activity will accumulate visible scratches on a high-polish band within months. Brushed and matte finishes are more forgiving — incidental scratches blend into the existing texture rather than reading as disruptions to a smooth surface. For daily-wear bands in active use, the less reflective finish options age better and require less frequent professional polishing.

Comfort fit refers not to the surface finish but to the interior profile of the band. A standard band has a flat interior that sits with two sharp edges against the finger. A comfort-fit band has a domed or rounded interior that contacts the finger on a curved surface, reducing the pressure sensation at the band edges. For men who find rings uncomfortable or have not worn jewelry before, comfort-fit interior significantly reduces the adjustment period.

### What People
## Ask Us

**Should the men's band match the engagement ring?**
Coordination is more useful than matching. The bands should share at least one material characteristic — metal type, or at minimum metal color — to read as a set. They do not need to be identical or even close in design. A platinum men's band with a brushed finish pairs well with a platinum diamond engagement ring, even if the two designs are completely different. The metal provides the visual connection.

**What width is most practical for a man who works with his hands?**
For men who regularly work with their hands — construction, medical fields, cooking, athletic training — 4mm to 6mm is the most practical range. Narrower bands are less likely to catch on equipment, less likely to create a pressure point when the hand is gripped around a tool, and cause fewer heat management concerns in environments where metal rings carry thermal risk. Wherever thermal or physical catching risk is a genuine workplace issue, a band should be removed before work.

**Can a men's band include diamonds?**
Yes. Diamond-set men's bands are more common than they were twenty years ago, and the design range has expanded significantly. A single row of channel-set diamonds across the band face is the most conservative option — clearly diamond but not overtly decorative. Channel-set baguette bands have a linear, architectural character that reads as masculine and minimal. Pavé-set bands are available and are appropriate for men who want significant sparkle in a band context.

**Is platinum the right choice for a man who exercises regularly?**
Platinum handles exercise context well — it is the most corrosion-resistant precious metal, is not damaged by sweat, and its wear patina disguises incidental surface marks. The one consideration for active wearers is weight: platinum is significantly heavier than gold at equivalent dimensions, and some men find the extra weight noticeable during athletic activity. Trying the band at the intended weight before purchase matters.

**How far in advance should we order the men's band?**
For a custom or made-to-order men's band, allow six to eight weeks from the design consultation to delivery. If the band is a standard design from existing inventory in the correct width and metal, allow two to three weeks for sizing and finishing. Do not leave the men's band order until after the women's engagement ring is finalized — the two pieces should be coordinated from the beginning, which means the men's design conversation should happen at the same time as the engagement ring conversation.
