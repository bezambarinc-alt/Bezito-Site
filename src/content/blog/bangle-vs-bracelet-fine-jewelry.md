*A bangle is a rigid structure that conducts sound, creates movement, and holds its position on the arm — a bracelet is a flexible structure that conforms to the wrist and disappears into the skin.*

---

### The Bangle
## What the Rigid Form Gives You and What It Costs

A bangle is a rigid ring of metal — gold, platinum, or silver — that slides over the hand and sits on the wrist or lower forearm. It does not flex, does not have a clasp in most configurations, and does not adjust in size. The fixed diameter must be large enough to pass over the knuckles of the hand during fitting but not so large that it slides off the narrower wrist once placed. This geometry constraint is the primary fitting challenge of the bangle category.

What the rigid form provides is presence and movement. A gold bangle in 18k yellow gold slides and catches light as the arm moves — the motion is visible at distance in a way that a conforming bracelet is not. Multiple bangles worn together produce sound — the soft percussion of gold on gold is a sensory characteristic that many wearers find appealing and others find distracting, which is a useful thing to know about yourself before building a bangle collection.

Bangles are the more democratic wrist piece in terms of occasion. A plain gold bangle is appropriate from a work environment to a formal dinner to a beach house breakfast because it reads as effortless rather than formal. A heavily diamond-set bangle is a statement piece with narrower occasion range. The unset gold bangle in a well-considered gauge and profile is one of the most versatile pieces in fine jewelry.

### The Bracelet
## What Flexibility and a Clasp Make Possible

A bracelet — in the sense of a flexible wrist piece with a clasp — fits precisely because it can be opened at the clasp and fastened at the correct length for the individual wrist. A tennis bracelet, a flex bracelet, a gold link bracelet, and a charm bracelet are all bracelet forms in this category. They conform to the wrist when fastened and move with the wrist's natural flexion rather than against it.

The clasp is both the advantage and the responsibility of bracelet ownership. The advantage is precise fit and the ability to remove the piece easily and securely. The responsibility is that the clasp is a mechanical component that experiences repeated stress and must be inspected regularly. A bangle owner has essentially no clasp maintenance to manage. A bracelet owner should inspect the clasp mechanism once or twice a year and have it serviced before the locking mechanism shows any looseness.

Flexibility also allows a bracelet to carry more material at a given apparent size. A flex bracelet — using a series of linked elements that articulate independently — can carry multiple rows of diamonds, substantial total weight, and significant stone surface area while lying flat on the wrist because the linked construction allows it to conform. A bangle at equivalent diamond coverage would need to be substantially larger in diameter to allow the hand to pass through, changing its proportional character.

### The Decision
## Matching the Form to the Wrist and the Life

Wrist proportions should inform the choice between bangle and bracelet. Narrow wrists — under 6.5 inches in circumference — typically wear bangles more successfully in smaller inner diameter configurations, and narrower gauge profiles. A heavy gauge bangle on a very narrow wrist reads as disproportionate, overloading a small frame. A fine gauge bangle in 2.0mm to 3.0mm wire reads as refined. A tennis bracelet on a narrow wrist can be adjusted to fit precisely, which bangles cannot.

Wider wrists — above 7 inches — can carry both forms easily. A wider wrist gives a bangle room to move without sliding off the hand during active use, and provides enough surface area for a wider-gauge bangle profile without the piece looking as though it is covering the entire wrist.

Daily activity should also factor into the decision. A bangle worn during keyboard work will slide to the forearm repeatedly, which some people tolerate and others find genuinely disruptive to concentration. A bracelet fastened at the correct wrist circumference stays in position. For a client who works at a desk all day, a tennis bracelet or other clasped bracelet typically earns more daily wear than a bangle that requires constant repositioning.

The two categories are not mutually exclusive. Many clients wear a clasped tennis bracelet alongside one or two bangles, which provides the best of both properties — precise diamond placement from the tennis bracelet and movement and sound from the bangles. This is one of the most successful bracelet combinations in the fine jewelry wardrobe.

### What People
## Ask Us

**How do I measure for a bangle?**
Measure the circumference of your hand at the widest point — across the knuckles when the fingers are held together and the hand is flat. The bangle's inner diameter must exceed this measurement to pass over the hand. For most adults, a hand circumference of 7 to 8 inches requires a bangle inner diameter of 2.4 to 2.6 inches. Add 0.1 to 0.2 inches for comfort on wider diameter bangles.

**Can bangles be resized?**
Solid metal bangles can be cut, sized, and resoldered if the profile allows for it. Simple round-profile bangles are resizable. Highly decorated or diamond-set bangles are often not resizable because the sizing process would interrupt stone settings or decorative work. It is better to size a bangle correctly at purchase than to attempt resizing later.

**What gauge bangle is best for daily wear?**
For gold bangles worn daily, a wall thickness or wire diameter of 3.0mm to 5.0mm in 18k gold provides enough structural rigidity to hold shape without bending under normal use. Thinner bangles — below 2.5mm — are more delicate and more susceptible to denting or bending if caught on a hard surface. Thicker bangles are heavier and make a stronger visual statement.

**Can I wear a bangle and a watch on the same wrist?**
Yes, but the bangle should sit above the watch rather than below it, to prevent the bangle from sliding over the watch crystal and scratching it. The wrist real estate between the watch and the hand — typically an inch or less — is too tight for comfortable bangle movement. Place the bangle on the forearm side of the watch, and it will slide freely without contact.

**What is a hinged bangle?**
A hinged bangle has a hinge at one point on its circumference and opens like a clamshell, which allows it to be placed on the wrist without passing over the hand. This solves the fitting problem for wrists where the hand-to-wrist size ratio makes a standard bangle difficult to fit. Hinged bangles require a secure snap closure at the opening point, which should be inspected periodically to confirm the snap mechanism holds under pressure.
