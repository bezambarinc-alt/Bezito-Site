### The Selection
## How to Decide What Travels and What Stays Home

The most important decision in traveling with fine jewelry is made before you pack anything: which pieces are worth the risk of travel. Risk in this context has two components — the physical risk of loss, theft, or damage, and the stress of responsibility for pieces of financial and sentimental value in an environment you do not control. Both risks increase with every piece you bring.

The practical travel jewelry selection answers these questions for each piece: Will I actually wear this, or am I bringing it because I might want to? Can I replace it if it is lost? Does its value justify the cognitive overhead of worrying about it for the duration of the trip? A piece that answers no to the first question, yes to the second, and raises uncertainty on the third probably stays home. A piece that you will wear daily, that has irreplaceable sentimental value, and that is adequately insured for the travel context travels with you.

High-value heirloom pieces — pieces of significant financial value, pieces with irreplaceable stones, pieces with no insurance coverage or coverage that excludes travel — should stay home. The sentimental argument for bringing an irreplaceable piece on a trip is almost always outweighed by the sentimental consequences of losing it. A high-resolution photograph of the piece, plus the memory of wearing it, travels free.

For most travel purposes, a well-chosen three-piece selection serves better than a larger collection: one pair of earrings that works across multiple dress codes, one necklace or pendant, and one bracelet or additional ring. These three pieces, combined with the jewelry you are wearing when you arrive, cover most occasions and fit easily in a compact jewelry roll.

### The Logistics
## How to Carry Fine Jewelry Through Airports and International Borders

Fine jewelry must travel in carry-on luggage, not checked baggage. This is not a preference — it is a practical necessity. Checked baggage is the most theft-vulnerable environment in modern travel; insurance claims for jewelry stolen from checked bags are common and frequently contested. Keep every piece on your person or in your carry-on from door to door.

Airport security screening requires you to remove jewelry that may trigger metal detection. Most fine jewelry — rings, necklaces, bracelets — will trigger standard metal detectors if the total metal mass is above the threshold. Having a small compact container in your carry-on bag makes the removal and replacement of pieces during screening faster and lower-risk than carrying pieces loose in your pocket. A small zippered pouch in an accessible compartment of your carry-on works. The thirty seconds of organization during screening significantly reduces the chance of leaving a piece in a screening bin.

International travel with fine jewelry requires attention to customs regulations. Bringing personal jewelry in and out of most countries for personal use is not subject to duty, but the distinction between "personal jewelry" and "goods for sale" depends on quantity and value. Very large quantities of fine jewelry, or carrying pieces without wearing them, can invite customs scrutiny. Keep documentation of original purchase for any high-value piece traveling internationally, and be prepared to demonstrate that the pieces are for personal use.

### The Hotel
## The Highest-Risk Hours of Any Trip

Hotels are the most vulnerable environment for jewelry during travel. A hotel room that appears locked and private is accessed routinely by housekeeping, maintenance, and in theft scenarios, unauthorized entrants. Jewelry left on a bathroom counter, beside the bed, or on a dresser is accessible to anyone who enters the room during the day.

The hotel room safe is better than leaving pieces on open surfaces, but hotel safes have known vulnerabilities — standard override codes exist for most common models, and their use is well understood in the theft-for-hire communities that target high-value travelers. For any piece you cannot afford to lose, the safest storage during a hotel stay is on your person, in your secured carry-on bag with the room, or in the hotel's main safe deposit facility at the front desk, which is separate from the room safes and carries a chain-of-custody log.

Do not leave fine jewelry in your hotel room overnight during beach or pool days, even in the room safe. The theft risk in resort environments is highest during daytime hours when guests are at the beach and rooms are accessible to staff. Either wear the jewelry to the beach (with the attendant risk of water, sun, and physical activity) or leave it at home. Most resort environments do not call for fine diamond jewelry, which is a useful frame for the selection decision before the trip begins.

### What People
## Ask Us

**Will my home jewelry insurance cover pieces while traveling internationally?**
It depends on the policy. Standard homeowner's jewelry riders often restrict coverage to losses within the home or within the United States. Dedicated jewelry insurance policies from specialty insurers typically cover pieces worldwide. Read the geographic coverage clause of your policy before any international trip with fine jewelry, and call your insurer if you are unclear.

**Can I ship fine jewelry to a destination rather than carrying it?**
Yes, for high-value pieces that would create airport or customs complications. Shipping must be via a carrier that offers declared-value coverage (FedEx, UPS, and some specialty fine art shippers), with the piece insured for its full replacement value. Ship to a secure address — not an unattended hotel room — and use the fastest available service to minimize the time the piece is in transit. Never ship fine jewelry through standard postal services.

**What should I wear through airport security to minimize delays?**
Wear pieces that you are not going to remove — embedded pieces, pieces with sentimental significance that you would not want to leave in a security bin. Put anything that you would be distressed to lose into your carry-on container before you reach the security lane. Plan for a sixty-second stop to reorganize after security rather than trying to remove and replace pieces at the conveyor.

**How should I store jewelry when swimming or at the beach?**
Lock it in your carry-on bag in the hotel room and do not bring it to the beach or pool. Saltwater and chlorinated water are both actively damaging to fine jewelry — chlorine attacks gold alloys, saltwater promotes corrosion at metal junctions, and sand is abrasive enough to scratch gold surfaces. The only jewelry appropriate for beach or pool wear is specifically fabricated for water resistance using non-reactive materials.

**What documentation should I bring when traveling with jewelry?**
Carry a digital copy of the insurance rider or dedicated policy coverage, photographs of each piece, and appraisal documentation. Store these documents separately from the jewelry — in your email or cloud storage — so that if the pieces are lost or stolen, the documentation remains accessible for the claim. A police report from the jurisdiction of the loss is required by most insurers.
