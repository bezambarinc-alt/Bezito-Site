### The Color Question
## What Makes One Aquamarine Better Than Another

Aquamarine is a variety of beryl, the same mineral family that produces emerald. Its color comes from trace iron, and unlike emerald, which is almost always heavily included, aquamarine grows in large, clean crystals. That cleanness is part of the stone's identity. A fine aquamarine should be eye-clean — visually free of inclusions — and most commercial-grade stones meet that standard without difficulty. Clarity is rarely where the buying decision gets complicated.

Color is where selection matters. The ideal aquamarine is a medium-dark, pure blue with no visible green modifier. The trade calls this "Santa Maria blue" after the historic Brazilian deposit that first produced stones of that intensity. True Santa Maria material is no longer commercially available in quantity; the name has become a quality descriptor rather than a geographic one. What you are looking for is saturation without darkness — a stone that holds its blue across the full face without going gray or navy under the surface.

Lighter, blue-green aquamarines are far more common and cost significantly less. They are still beautiful stones. The decision is whether the blue you see in natural light is the blue you want on your hand every day. Blue-green aquamarine looks distinctly different from pure blue aquamarine, and that difference is most visible in indoor lighting, where cooler tones tend to fade and green components become more apparent.

### Durability for Daily Wear
## How Aquamarine Performs in an Engagement Ring

Beryl registers 7.5 to 8 on the Mohs scale, which places aquamarine comfortably above quartz — the mineral in most household dust. It will not accumulate surface scratches as quickly as stones softer than quartz, but it will show wear over years if the setting design allows the girdle or pavilion to contact rough surfaces regularly. Beryl also has imperfect cleavage, meaning a sharp blow in the wrong direction can chip a facet edge. A bezel setting or a four-prong mount with protective head placement is preferable to a low bezel or flush channel that leaves the girdle exposed.

Aquamarine is stable in light — it will not fade with ultraviolet exposure the way some amethyst does. It is sensitive to heat; avoid steam cleaning and prolonged exposure to very hot water. Ultrasonic cleaning is safe for untreated stones. Most aquamarines on the market are not treated; unlike emerald, which is almost universally oiled, aquamarine reaches gem quality without enhancement in most cases. A certificate noting "no indications of heating" adds modest value but is not essential for aquamarine the way it is for sapphire.

### Setting and Style
## Designing the Right Ring for a Blue Stone

Aquamarine reads differently in different light conditions, and the setting can help or hurt that. Platinum and white gold amplify the stone's cool blue register; yellow gold warms it and can push the blue-green stones into more olive territory. Rose gold creates a complementary contrast that some clients find striking — the warm metal against the cool stone — though it narrows the stone's color range in artificial light.

Step cuts are the natural match for aquamarine. The emerald cut in particular suits the stone's transparency and its geometric regularity; the long parallel facets of an emerald cut create a depth effect in clear material that brilliant cuts do not. Oval and cushion brilliant cuts are also popular and produce more light return than step cuts, which is useful if the stone is lighter in color and needs visual brightness to appear vibrant. Round brilliant is less common in aquamarine simply because the stone's rectangular or hexagonal crystal habit makes other shapes more efficient to cut.

### Size and Pricing
## What Your Budget Buys in Aquamarine

Aquamarine is one of the genuinely accessible fine gemstones. Commercial-quality stones in the two-to-five carat range are available for $100 to $300 per carat. Fine, deep-blue material with strong saturation runs $500 to $1,500 per carat in comparable sizes. Exceptional stones — vivid Santa Maria-blue material above five carats from known deposits — can push above $3,000 per carat, but that ceiling is far below what comparable quality costs in sapphire or emerald.

The practical implication is that budget which would buy a one-carat sapphire can buy a three-or-four carat aquamarine of comparable or better quality. For clients who want a presence on the hand without the engagement-ring diamond price structure, aquamarine is the most rational choice among blue stones. The stone photographs beautifully and holds its appearance reliably over decades with basic care. It is not the rarest stone in the case, but rarity and beauty are different things, and aquamarine has always known the difference.

### What People
## Ask Us

**Is aquamarine durable enough for an engagement ring?**
Aquamarine is hard enough for daily wear — it rates 7.5 to 8 on the Mohs scale — but it has imperfect cleavage and can chip if struck sharply on the facet edges. A protective setting style, such as a bezel or a four-prong with raised heads, reduces that risk significantly. Worn with reasonable care, an aquamarine engagement ring will last generations.

**What is the difference between aquamarine and blue topaz?**
Both are blue stones, but they are different minerals entirely. Aquamarine is a beryl; blue topaz is a topaz, and most commercial blue topaz is irradiated and heat-treated to produce its color. Fine aquamarine has a more complex, often deeper blue than treated topaz. Under magnification and spectroscopic testing, they are easily distinguished. On the hand, a fine aquamarine typically outperforms blue topaz in saturation and depth.

**Does aquamarine fade over time?**
Natural aquamarine is stable and does not fade with normal light exposure. Some pale aquamarines have been heat-treated to remove green overtones; that treatment is permanent and stable. The stone will not change color in sunlight or under indoor lighting over the decades of wear.

**How large should an aquamarine engagement ring be?**
The stone's relatively modest price means you can afford to go larger than you might with diamond or sapphire. A three-to-five carat aquamarine reads as substantial on most hand sizes and costs a fraction of a comparable diamond. For lighter-colored material, larger size actually improves the visual experience by giving the color more surface area to develop.

**Can I clean aquamarine at home?**
Warm water, mild dish soap, and a soft brush are all you need. Rinse thoroughly and dry with a lint-free cloth. Avoid steam cleaning, which can stress the stone thermally. Ultrasonic cleaners are generally safe for untreated aquamarine, but when in doubt, use the soap-and-water method.
