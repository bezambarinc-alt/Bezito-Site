*Most people think garnet means dark red — but garnet spans orange, yellow-green, vivid green, and color-change varieties that the gem trade prizes as seriously as the finest sapphires.*

---

### The Garnet Family
## More Species Than Most Buyers Know Exist

The word garnet describes a mineral group, not a single stone. The major species that appear in fine jewelry are pyrope (deep red), almandine (darker red-purple), spessartine (orange to orange-red), grossular (which includes the vivid green tsavorite and the yellowish-green grossularite), and andradite (which includes the brilliant, high-dispersion demantoid). Each has a distinct color range, set of properties, and price trajectory. Understanding which species you are looking at is the first discipline of buying garnet well.

Rhodolite garnet, which is not a distinct species but a trade name for a pinkish-red to purple-red pyrope-almandine mixture, is one of the most commercially significant garnets in fine jewelry. It occupies the space between the dark, sometimes opaque quality of deep almandine and the vivid, saturated ruby-like color of the finest pyrope, and it does so at prices that make it accessible for significant center stones. The color range — raspberry, rose-red, purple-red — is genuinely attractive and photographs well.

### The Two Varieties Worth Knowing at a Premium Level
## Tsavorite and Demantoid

Tsavorite is grossular garnet from East Africa — principally Tanzania and Kenya — colored by vanadium and chromium to produce a vivid, pure green that many gemologists consider the closest natural equivalent to a fine Colombian emerald, without the inclusions and treatment complexity. Fine tsavorite in the one-to-two carat range with strong saturation commands serious prices, and the stone deserves the attention it receives from sophisticated collectors. The supply of fine tsavorite is genuinely limited, and the color, when it is right, is extraordinary.

Demantoid, the green variety of andradite garnet from Russia and Namibia, has the highest dispersion of any colored gemstone — higher than diamond. The result is a fire and brilliance that seems disproportionate to the stone's modest size. Demantoid typically comes in smaller sizes because the rough is limited, but even a half-carat demantoid in a ring makes its presence known. Russian demantoid often contains characteristic horsetail inclusions — fine, curved rutile fibers — that are actually considered proof of origin and add value rather than detracting from it.

### Garnet for Engagement Rings
## What Works and What to Know About Durability

Most garnet species are hard enough for regular jewelry wear. Tsavorite, demantoid, and pyrope all fall between 6.5 and 7.5 on the Mohs scale. This places them below sapphire and ruby in hardness, which means they will accumulate surface scratches over years of daily wear more readily. For a ring that will be worn every day for decades, the setting needs to protect the stone, and the client needs to be realistic about maintenance and re-polishing over time.

The deep red varieties — pyrope, rhodolite, almandine — are typically available in sizes large enough for a center stone at prices that are genuinely accessible. A three-carat rhodolite with good color and clarity is a fraction of the price of a comparable pink sapphire. The difference is durability, rarity, and the depth of the collector market behind it. For buyers who value color and presence over investment potential, garnet makes an honest argument.

### Spessartine and the Orange Category
## The Mandarin Garnet and Its Market

Spessartine garnet from Namibia and Nigeria, sold under the trade name Mandarin garnet, produces an orange of remarkable intensity — more vivid and saturated than most orange sapphires, at a fraction of the price. The color is warm, cheerful, and distinct. Fine Mandarin garnet in the two-to-four carat range with strong orange saturation and good clarity is one of the more exciting purchases available in the colored stone market for buyers willing to explore outside the traditional ruby-sapphire-emerald triumvirate.

Spessartine is also found in less vivid orange-red grades at many price points below the top Mandarin tier. The quality spectrum is wide, and seeing stones in person to evaluate the saturation of the orange component is essential.

### What People
## Ask Us

**What is the most valuable type of garnet?**
By per-carat price, demantoid garnet from Russia and tsavorite from East Africa are the most expensive, with top-quality stones in significant sizes trading at prices comparable to fine sapphire and emerald. Fine Mandarin spessartine above two carats with strong orange color also commands strong premiums. The common dark red almandine and commercial rhodolite are far more affordable.

**Does garnet require special care?**
Garnet is generally stable under normal cleaning conditions. Ultrasonic cleaning is safe for most garnet varieties except demantoid, which should be cleaned gently by hand. Avoid rapid temperature changes. The main care consideration for a garnet ring is that harder surfaces will scratch the stone over time, so periodic re-polishing by a jeweler is worthwhile for daily-wear pieces.

**Is garnet a birthstone?**
Garnet is the traditional January birthstone, though in practice this designation applies across the full garnet family. A tsavorite — vivid green — is still garnet, still appropriate as a January stone, and is a genuinely exceptional gift for buyers willing to move beyond the default dark-red association.

**Can garnet be found in colors other than red?**
Yes, the full garnet color range includes orange (spessartine), vivid green (tsavorite), yellowish-green (grossular), greenish-yellow (Mali garnet), and purple-red (rhodolite). The one color garnet cannot achieve naturally is blue — though a color-change garnet that shifts from green to red-purple under different light sources is known in the trade and is exceptionally rare.

**How large can a garnet center stone be in a ring?**
Rhodolite and pyrope are commonly available in sizes up to 10 carats at accessible prices. Tsavorite becomes very expensive above two carats and rare above five. Demantoid is almost never found in commercial sizes above two carats. Spessartine Mandarin material is most commonly available between one and four carats in fine quality. Size availability varies significantly by species.
