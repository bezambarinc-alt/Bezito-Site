The Beginning

A Diamond Visionary

Born in Turkey and trained in the art of diamond cutting, Bez Ambar arrived in Los Angeles with an idea that would reshape an entire industry. He believed diamonds could be cut differently — that brilliance didn't have to follow tradition.

In 1982, he proved it. The Princess cut was born — a square brilliant that combined the fire of a round with the modern geometry the world didn't know it wanted. It became one of the most popular diamond shapes globally.

But Bez didn't stop there.

Innovation Continues

The Blaze, Elysian & Alara Cuts

The Blaze cut followed — a patented design with 13 precisely aligned facets that create an unmistakable starburst of light visible to the naked eye. No other house in the world can produce it.

Then came the Elysian Cut™ — a family of original step-cut geometries — and the Alara cut, now in the patent process, engineered to maximize fire and brilliance in a way that defies convention. Every stone is a testament to what happens when craft meets obsession.

Three proprietary cuts. Three things no competitor can replicate. That's the Bez Ambar difference.

Milestones

A Legacy of Firsts

1979

Ambar Diamonds is Founded

Bez Ambar establishes his atelier in Los Angeles — the workshop where every innovation since has been born.

1982

The Princess Cut is Born

Bez and his partners create the Quadrillion — the cut the world came to know as the Princess — forever changing the diamond industry.

1980s

Global Recognition

The Princess cut becomes one of the world's most popular diamond shapes. Bez Ambar gains international acclaim.

2003

The Blaze Cut

Bez patents the Blaze cut — 13 perfectly aligned facets creating an unmistakable pattern of light.

2010s

The Alara Cut

The latest proprietary cut is unveiled, designed to maximize fire and brilliance beyond anything previously achieved.

2026
