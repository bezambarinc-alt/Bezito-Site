The Inventor

Bez Ambar

Israeli-American diamond cutter and jewelry designer. He arrived in Los Angeles in 1979 with a belief that diamonds could be cut differently. In 1982, he proved it. The cut he invented became the Princess cut — now the most widely sold diamond shape in the world. He has spent forty-six years proving that point was not a beginning. It was a method.

The Work

Bez Ambar founded Ambar Diamonds Inc. in Los Angeles in 1979, trained in the discipline of diamond cutting and with a clear conviction about what brilliance could look like. Three years after founding, he introduced the Quadrillion® cut — a square brilliant that captured the fire of a round diamond within a modern, architectural silhouette. The trade called it the Princess cut. The name stuck. The cut spread across the industry and is now the most recognizable shape in contemporary fine jewelry.

In 1985, the Quadrillion® received the DeBeers Award — an industry recognition for its contribution to diamond design. Bez did not stop there. In 1988, he introduced the Laserset®, a rimless and prongless setting for square-cut diamonds that allowed the stone to appear suspended in the hand, uninterrupted. In 1992, the Boundless setting brought the same logic to round diamonds. In 1999, what became known as the Micro-Pavé setting was introduced — a technique that has since entered the standard vocabulary of fine jewelry.

In 2003, the Blaze® cut was patented. Thirteen precisely aligned facets produce a starburst of light visible to the naked eye under white light. The effect is specific to the Blaze® geometry — it cannot be replicated in another cut. The Elysian Cut™ followed: an elongated diamond geometry engineered to intensify the interplay of facets along its axis. Each proprietary cut represents not a departure from the tradition of diamond cutting but a deeper application of its principles.

A Record

1979
Bez Ambar arrives in Los Angeles and founds Ambar Diamonds Inc. His background is in diamond cutting. His intention is to work differently.
1982
The Quadrillion® cut is introduced — a square brilliant that combines round-diamond fire with a modern square silhouette. The trade adopts it as the Princess cut. It becomes the world's most popular diamond shape.
1985
DeBeers Award — the ATW Quadrillion® ring receives recognition from DeBeers, one of the industry's most authoritative honors.
1988
The Laserset® — a rimless, prongless setting for square-cut diamonds. The stone appears to float in the hand, uninterrupted by any metal at its edges.
1992
The Boundless setting — the same suspended logic as the Laserset®, adapted for round diamonds. No rim. No prong. Only stone.
1999
A new pavé technique enters the atelier. The industry eventually names it Micro-Pavé — a word now in common use, originating in this workshop.
2003
The Blaze® cut is patented. Thirteen aligned facets produce a visible starburst of light — an optical effect specific to this geometry and reproducible nowhere else.
2015
The Divine Cut® — a round brilliant engineered for a specific quality of dispersion, continuing the atelier's work on light behavior within the cut stone.

Proprietary Cuts

Since 1982
Quadrillion® — The Princess Cut
The square brilliant that changed the industry. Quadrillion® is the original name; Princess cut is what the world calls it now. The fire of a round diamond, locked in a geometric square. Still the most widely sold diamond shape globally.
Patented 2003
Blaze®
Thirteen precisely aligned facets produce a starburst of light visible to the naked eye under white light. The effect is a function of the Blaze® geometry specifically — it does not occur in any other cut. A registered trademark and active patent.
Elysian Cut™
Elysian™
An elongated diamond geometry that intensifies facet interplay along the stone's principal axis. Each Elysian stone is individually assessed for the particular quality of light it produces. The name is a Bez Ambar trademark.
