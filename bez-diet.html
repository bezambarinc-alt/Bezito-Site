The Bez Plan — Modified Esselstyn for Your Heart

CAC 581 · LAD 50–69% · Lp(a) 10 (excellent) · Family history: father had triple bypass · Goal: stabilize and modestly regress plaque · 2026-05-15
The deal: Esselstyn's strict protocol works (peer-reviewed, decades of data) but it's brutal. This version keeps 80% of the cardiac benefit and is sustainable in real life — Israeli-Mediterranean friendly, work-around-the-jewelry-business friendly, and lets you keep coffee and a glass of red wine. Pair this with your high-intensity statin to push LDL <55 and ApoB <60. Diet alone is not enough at CAC 581.
The Bez Rules — Modified Esselstyn
Always (the non-negotiables)
  • No red meat, no processed meat. Zero. This is the single biggest move.
  • No dairy. No milk, cheese, butter, yogurt, ice cream. Use unsweetened soy or oat milk.
  • No fried foods. No falafel, no schnitzel, no fries, no donuts.
  • 6 servings of leafy greens per day. Spinach, arugula, kale, chard, romaine, parsley. Big handful counts as one.
  • Beans or lentils every day. Hummus counts (made with minimal oil).
  • Whole grains only. No white bread, no white rice, no white pasta. Whole-wheat pita, brown rice, freekeh, bulgur, quinoa, oats.
Allowed (where this differs from strict Esselstyn)
  • Olive oil — 1 tablespoon per day max. Strict Esselstyn says zero. The PREDIMED trial (8,000 patients, 5 years) showed Mediterranean diet with olive oil cut cardiac events 30%. The evidence supports moderate olive oil. Use it for finishing, not frying.
  • Nuts — small handful daily after week 2. Walnuts especially. Real evidence of cardiac benefit.
  • Fish — 1–2× per week. Wild salmon, sardines, mackerel. Strict Esselstyn says no. Mediterranean evidence says yes. Your call.
  • Skinless chicken breast — 1× per week max. Grilled or baked only. No skin, no breading, no frying. Strict Esselstyn says no; observational data shows chicken is cardiac-neutral. Don't let it become the daily default — that's how it crowds out beans and lentils.
  • Eggs — 2 per week max. Yolk and all. Not zero, not three.
  • Coffee — yes, 1–2 cups before noon. Evidence is neutral-to-positive for cardiac outcomes.
  • Red wine — 1 glass (4–5 oz), 3–4 nights per week max. Not the morning. Not every night.
  • Dark chocolate — 1 square (1 oz) 70%+ per day if you want.
Never
  • Sugar-sweetened beverages (soda, sweet drinks, sweetened iced tea)
  • Anything with the words partially hydrogenated on the label
  • Margarine, vegetable shortening, palm oil
  • Bourekas, pastry, croissants, donuts (refined-flour + fat bombs)
  • Deli meat, hot dogs, salami, pastrami, jerky
A Day in the Bez Kitchen

Breakfast — target ~20g protein

Option A: Steel-cut oats + berries + walnuts + cinnamon + soy milk + 2 tbsp hemp hearts. (~22g)
Option B: Whole-wheat pita + hummus (½ cup) + Israeli salad + tahini. (~18g)
Option C: Green smoothie — spinach, banana, berries, 3 tbsp hemp hearts, soy milk, ground flax. (~20g)
Option D: Tofu scramble (firm tofu, turmeric, spinach, tomato) + whole-grain toast. (~25g)
Drink: Black coffee or green tea.

Lunch — target ~35g protein

Option A: Bowl — brown rice or freekeh + 1 cup lentils + roasted veg + greens + tahini-lemon. (~32g)
Option B: Mezze plate — hummus, baba, tabbouleh, edamame, whole-wheat pita, olives. (~28g)
Option C: Lentil soup (2 cups) + big mixed salad with chickpeas + whole-grain bread. (~30g)
Option D: Tempeh stir-fry (3 oz tempeh + broccoli + bell pepper + ginger-garlic) over brown rice. (~30g)

Dinner — target ~35–40g protein

Option A: Wild salmon (4 oz, baked) + sweet potato + sautéed kale. (~30g)
Option B: Stuffed peppers — quinoa, 1 cup black beans, tomato, spinach. (~25g)
Option C: Chickpea & cauliflower curry over brown rice + broccoli. (~22g)
Option D: Whole-wheat pasta + marinara + 1 cup white beans + roasted veg. (~28g)
Option E: Mujadara (lentils + brown rice + caramelized onion) + Israeli salad. (~25g)
Option F: Grilled skinless chicken breast (4 oz) + big mixed salad + roasted vegetables + side of lentils. (~45g) · Max 1× per week.

Snacks — target ~10–15g protein

• Apple + 6–8 walnuts (5g)
• Carrots + ¼ cup hummus (5g)
• Edamame, 1 cup (17g) ← protein-dense
• Roasted chickpeas, ½ cup (10g)
• Hemp-heart yogurt: ½ cup soy yogurt + 2 tbsp hemp hearts (12g)
• Dark chocolate square (70%+) (1g)
Protein target: 100–130 g/day. Older adults in fat-loss mode need more protein than the standard 0.8 g/kg — that figure was set for 20-year-olds. We're going for 1.2–1.4 g/kg of goal weight to preserve muscle. A typical day above (Option A breakfast + A lunch + A dinner + edamame snack) = ~104g. Add a second bean serving or 1 egg → 115g. Easy to hit.

What to Track
  • Labs at 3 months: LDL (target <55), ApoB (target <60), hs-CRP, A1c, fasting glucose, fasting insulin
  • Body: Weight weekly. Blood pressure 2× per week (morning).
  • Daily: Did you hit 6 servings of greens? Beans? 30 min movement?
  • Symptoms: Any chest pressure, shortness of breath, jaw/arm pain on exertion — call cardiology same day.
Realistic expectations. Esselstyn's published data shows single-digit percent plaque regression over years — not 50% in 3 months like Kevin Ham claims. What this diet does reliably do: drops LDL 15–30%, drops ApoB, drops hs-CRP (inflammation), drops blood pressure, drops A1c. The plaque you have today is mostly stabilized, not erased. Stabilized plaque doesn't rupture — and ruptured plaque is what causes heart attacks. That's the win.
This is not a substitute for your statin. At CAC 581, lifestyle alone is undertreatment. Diet drops LDL ~15–30%. A high-intensity statin drops it another 50%. You need both. If you're not on Rosuvastatin 20–40 or Atorvastatin 40–80, that's the conversation to have with your cardiologist this month.
