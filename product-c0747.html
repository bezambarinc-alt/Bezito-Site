Fancy Very Pink Oval

A Fancy Very Pink oval, five carats, lab-grown to the millimetre — thirteen and a half millimetres end to end, nine across, five point eight deep. The pink saturates uniform through the length of the crystal, table to culet, without the colour zoning a mined fancy pink is known for; lab-growth lets the pink lay down evenly, and the oval cut gives the eye a full run of colour from tip to tip. The stone chosen first, the setting chosen for the stone — the same principle Bez Ambar has held since 1979, the same hand, forty-seven years on.

Pink, End to End

The problem with a fancy pink is colour zoning. In a mined pink diamond the colour concentrates in bands or facets — the eye reads a bright pink at one end and a pale wash at the other, and the stone stops holding together as one piece. Lab-growth lets the pink saturate through the crystal uniform. The oval cut, elongated to thirteen and a half millimetres, gives the colour the full run of the stone to be read as one continuous pink. That is what this stone is engineered for, and it is why we chose to set it long.

Pink, All the Way Through

Every stone we set is chosen for how it holds its colour end to end, not just at the table. Lab-grown, uniform pink, cut to give the eye a full read.
