01 London · Mayfair
Glenn Spiro
G London — Est. 2014

The jeweler's jeweler. Twenty-five years in the trade — including running Christie's Jewelry Department — before opening a private atelier in the former studio of royal couturier Sir Norman Hartnell on Bruton Street, Mayfair. Works exclusively in exceptional, one-of-a-kind stones that take years to set. As exclusive as the field gets.

"There are a limited number of people who have the style, conviction, confidence and financial ability to own fabulous jewellery."
Location
26 Bruton Street, Mayfair, London — appointment only. Also St. Tropez, St. Barths, Bergdorf Goodman NYC
Output
Tiny. Individual pieces can take years to complete
Retail
Harrods London + Bergdorf Goodman NYC only
Notable
Assouline monograph published. Father-son operation — son Joe now works alongside
Rarity Technical Mastery Appointment Only Stone Sourcing Mayfair

02 New York · Madison Ave
Taffin
James de Givenchy — Est. 1996

James Claude Taffin de Givenchy — nephew of couturier Hubert de Givenchy. French-American, based entirely in New York where everything is designed and made. Came out of Christie's West Coast Jewelry Department, then Verdura. Wildly inventive: unexpected materials, graphic comic-book energy filtered through a museum sensibility. 80% of pieces are custom commissions. Genuinely art-object territory, not fashion-adjacent jewelry.

Location
501 Madison Avenue, NYC — appointment-only salon he designed himself; atelier in the same building
Signature
Statue of Liberty hallmark stamp on every piece
Output
One-of-a-kind. Roughly 80% custom commissions
Published
Rizzoli 400-page monograph, 2016 — extremely rare for a jeweler this private
Art Object Custom Commissions French-American Museum Sensibility NYC

03 Hong Kong · Central
Forms
Janover & Assor — Est. 2010

Founded by Tzvika Janover and Elad Assor — both from multi-generational diamond cutting families. Hidden inside a nondescript office building in Central Hong Kong with no signage. Collectors fly in from around the world. Walk-in vault lined with trays of exceptional stones: rare old rubies, untreated Colombian emeralds, champagne diamonds. Pieces emerge from playing with the actual stones until an idea forms. One of the few houses where the atelier and the vault exist under the same roof.

Location
2/F Hong Kong Diamond Exchange Building, 8-10 Duddell Street, Central — no signage
Output
Maximum 100 unique pieces per year
Philosophy
"Hardly a trace of metal visible." Metal serves the stone, never dominates it
DNA
Mughal and Indian jewel tradition filtered through contemporary technical innovation
Stone First Hidden Atelier Max 100 Pieces / Year Vault + Atelier Hong Kong

04 London · New Bond St
Jeremy Morris
David Morris — Est. 1962

Second-generation. David Morris founded the house in 1962 after training at Hatton Garden and graduating from the Central School of Arts and Crafts. Jeremy took over as managing director and principal designer in 2003. Big bold color, obsessive gemstone sourcing — suppliers give first look on exceptional stones. Innovating in materials (titanium alongside gold). The most retail-visible of the four, but still genuinely high jewelry with a working atelier above the Bond Street boutique.

Location
180 New Bond Street, London (Grade II* listed 1908 townhouse) + 12 boutiques worldwide
Heritage
De Beers Diamonds International Awards 1963 & 1964. Made the Miss World Crown. James Bond films. Elizabeth Taylor (via Richard Burton)
Clientele
Barbra Streisand, Oprah, Kate Winslet, Adele, Keira Knightley, Catherine Zeta-Jones
Scale
12 boutiques: London, Harrods, Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha, Hong Kong, Paris
Net-a-Porter
Sold on NAP — the only jeweler on this list with a major e-commerce presence there. NAP's jewelry roster includes Cartier, Tiffany, Messika, Chopard, Shay, Anita Ko. Their EIP (Extremely Important People) clients — $25K+/yr spenders, top 2% of customers — account for ~50% of all NAP revenue. Being listed = editorial validation + global reach to that exact buyer. NAP also publishes PORTER magazine, so brands get written up alongside being shoppable.

Common Thread

What These Four Share

Access
Appointment-only or near-invisible to the public. You have to know to find them.
Philosophy
Stone-first design. The gem dictates the piece, not the other way around.
Scale
Deliberately tiny output. Rarity is structural, not marketing.
Craft
Extraordinary technical mastery. Settings and techniques unavailable at any other scale.
Clientele
Collectors, not customers. People who understand what they're buying.
For Bez Ambar
Forms and Taffin are the closest analogs to where Bez Ambar is heading — stone-first, hidden, custom, collectible.
