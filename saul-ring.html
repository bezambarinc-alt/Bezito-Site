The Crossover

Two currents, one ring — a study in opposing light.

The Design

Every great ring resolves a tension. This one carries two.

The upper row runs east to west — long, step-faceted diamonds laid end-to-end along the band's curve, like still water held in a channel of white gold.

The lower row runs north to south — tapered baguettes splaying outward from the band like the spokes of a fan, gathering light at a different angle, on a band that lifts in a subtle wave of its own.

Where the two rows meet at the front of the ring, they form a single confluence — the design's signature. From above, two distinct light signatures cross. From the side, two currents pass under one hand.

Set entirely in 18-karat white gold, prong by prong, with BEZ engraved inside the shank — the maker's quiet signature on a piece designed for one wearer only.

A Note from Bez

Saul — this rendering shows the design executed in emerald-cut and tapered baguette diamonds. The same architecture translates very naturally to Ashoka-cut diamonds, which would lend the upper row an even softer, more sculptural silhouette. Before we settle on stones, I'd love to know if the design itself resonates with your daughter. The stones are the easy part. The form is the conversation.
