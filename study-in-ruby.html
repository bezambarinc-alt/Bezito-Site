A stone of this weight, in this colour, in this cut, asks a single question of its maker: what shall it wear?

Studying that question — slowly, by hand, in graphite and watercolour — is itself part of the art of making jewelry. The differences between two frames can be minute: the angle of a baguette, the depth of a halo, the breath between the stone and its setting.

In our trade, a subtle difference is sometimes a world apart.

What you have just seen is not a collection, but a study. One pair of cushion rubies — two to six carat — dressed several ways. Some frames are taken from collections already in the house; others are answers we drew on the way to finding the right one.
