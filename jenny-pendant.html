The Jenny Pendant

A museum-grade wearable encasement for a 1918 24¢ airmail invert — bottom-selvage single
Client: Eric  •  Custodian & maker: Bez  •  Design revision: D (all-gold)  •  Updated: August 13, 2026

IThe Concept

The stamp (27.0 × 31.0 × ≈0.09 mm) floats in an argon atmosphere between two UV-protected windows, touched by nothing under pressure. It sits in the opening of a clear archival mat that is a hair thicker than the stamp — the mat carries every gram of clamping force; the stamp carries none. A narrow PTFE ring squeezed between the two window panes forms the primary airtight seal (glass–PTFE–glass, the double-glazing principle). The whole stack drops into a gold tray closed by a gold frame and six gold screws turning in hidden steel threads. Everything is mechanical, cold, and reversible with hand tools. This is — at pendant scale — the same architecture the U.S. National Archives uses to encase the Declaration of Independence: machined case, floating document, inert gas, gaskets, screws.

VThe Rules (non-negotiable)

  • The stamp is never trimmed, hinged, taped, or touched by adhesive or metal. The selvage stays.
  • No adhesives anywhere in the sealed volume. Only Melinex, PTFE, window material, and argon live inside.
  • All hot & violent work (soldering, setting, polishing) is finished before the stamp enters. Sealing is cold.
  • The stamp is the last part in and the first part out. The capsule opens with hand tools only.
  • Handling: conservator or gloved hands + stamp tongs, perforation margin only.
  • Documentation: condition photos before sealing; expertization certificate stays with the piece; insurance confirmed in writing before custody.

VIAssembly Procedure

  1. Dress rehearsal first — full run with a worthless stamp. Magnetize the screwdriver. Find the awkward moments when nothing is at stake.
  2. Pre-condition all parts 24 h with silica gel; pre-fit and fog-test the capsule empty.
  3. Stage everything open in the glove bag; purge with argon low and slow ≥ 15–20 min (O₂ meter < 0.5% if available; argon is heavier than air and pools).
  4. Stack in the tray: face gasket → front pane (coated face in) → seal ring → mat → stamp (tongs, perf edge) → rear pane → face gasket → lid.
  5. Screws finger-tight, then small rounds in a cross pattern. Stop when resistance jumps — that is the panes landing flat on the mat. Snug, not strong.
  6. Fog test: chill ≈5 °C for 30 min, warm one face — any interior fog = open (in argon) and re-seat. No fog = sealed.
  7. Re-snug the screws the next day (PTFE relaxes). Photograph, log date/gas/procedure.
